Grade 9 Linear Systems & Relations: Official Process Cheat Sheet

1. Identifying Linear, Quadratic, & Exponential Models

📈 Linear

• Table: First differences ($\Delta y$) are constant when $x$ increments uniformly.

• Graph: Straight continuous line.

• Equation: Degree 1: $y = mx + b$.

🪃 Quadratic

• Table: First differences change, but second differences ($\Delta^2 y$) are constant.

• Graph: U-shaped or inverted parabola.

• Equation: Degree 2: $y = ax^2 + bx + c$.

🚀 Exponential

• Table: Constant ratio / multiplier between consecutive $y$-values: $\frac{y_{k+1}}{y_k} = \text{const}$.

• Graph: Rapidly curving growth or decay towards asymptote.

• Equation: $x$ is in the exponent: $y = a(b)^x$.

2. Slope & Slope-Intercept Formulation

  • Slope Formula: $$m = \frac{\text{rise}}{\text{run}} = \frac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1}$$
  • Special Slopes: Horizontal line $\rightarrow m = 0$ (equation: $y = c$). Vertical line $\rightarrow m = \text{undefined}$ (division by 0, equation: $x = c$).
  • Finding $y$-intercept ($b$): Once slope $m$ is determined, plug in any known coordinate $(x, y)$: $$b = y - mx$$
  • Equation of a Line: Write in standard slope-intercept form: $y = mx + b$.

3. Parallel & Perpendicular Line Relationships

Parallel Lines ($\parallel$)

Lines have the exact same slope ($m_1 = m_2$) but different $y$-intercepts ($b_1 \neq b_2$). They never meet.

Example: $y = 3x + 1$ and $y = 3x - 5$.

Perpendicular Lines ($\perp$)

Slopes are negative reciprocals ($m_1 \cdot m_2 = -1$). To find $m_2$, flip the fraction and negate sign:

Example: If $m_1 = -\frac{2}{3}$, then $m_2 = +\frac{3}{2}$.

4. Rearranging Standard Form into $y = mx + b$

  1. Isolate the $y$ term: Move $x$ and constant terms to the right side of the equals sign by changing their signs ($Ax + By + C = 0 \rightarrow By = -Ax - C$).
  2. Divide by the coefficient of $y$: Divide every single term by $B$: $$y = -\frac{A}{B}x - \frac{C}{B}$$
  3. Simplify all fractions: Ensure fractions are reduced to lowest terms and inspect signs carefully.

5. Linear Systems: Number of Solutions & POI

• 1 Unique Solution (Point of Intersection): Occurs when lines have different slopes ($m_1 \neq m_2$). The lines cross at exactly one $(x, y)$.

• No Solution: Occurs when lines have the same slope but different $y$-intercepts ($m_1 = m_2$, $b_1 \neq b_2$). Parallel lines never intersect.

• Infinitely Many Solutions: Occurs when both equations represent the exact same line ($m_1 = m_2$ and $b_1 = b_2$). Every point on the line is a solution.
